Demand & Supply M.Com

The core model of price determination. Equilibrium is where Qd = Qs. Shift the demand or supply curves and watch the new equilibrium update instantly.

Market Equilibrium

Law of Demand · Law of Supply · Shifts · Surplus & Shortage

Equilibrium

⚙ Variables

Demand Intercept (a) 200
Demand Slope (b) 1.5
Supply Intercept (c) 20
Supply Slope (d) 1.2
📍 Equilibrium
—
Demand: P = a − b·Q  |  Supply: P = c + d·Q
Q* = (a−c)/(b+d)    P* = c + d·Q*
Right-shift Demand (↑a): income↑, tastes, substitutes price↑, future expectations

Right-shift Supply (↓c): input cost↓, technology improvement, subsidy

Surplus: at P above P*, Qs > Qd — price falls back.
Shortage: at P below P*, Qd > Qs — price rises.

Production Function M.Com

Relates inputs (Labour, Capital) to output. The short run has a fixed factor; in the long run all factors vary. Shows Total, Average, and Marginal products.

Cost Curves M.Com

Simultaneously determines output (Y) and interest rate (r). IS curve: goods market equilibrium. LM curve: money market equilibrium. Fiscal/monetary policy shift the curves.

📍 Equilibrium
—
IS: r = (A − Y/m)/b    (A = autonomous spending)
LM: r = (kY − M/P)/h

Fiscal policy (G↑): IS shifts right → Y↑, r↑ (crowding out)

Phillips Curve M.Com PhD

Short-run trade-off between inflation and unemployment. Long-run Phillips curve is vertical at NAIRU (Natural Rate). Expectations shift the short-run curve.

Short-Run & Long-Run Phillips Curve

SRPC · LRPC · NAIRU · Adaptive Expectations · Stagflation

SRPC (shifted)
LRPC (NAIRU)

⚙ Variables

NAIRU (%) 5
Inflation expectations (π^e) 2
Sacrifice ratio (a) 0.5
Curve position
—
π = π^e − a(u − u*)
u* = NAIRU (Natural rate; LRPC is vertical here)
π^e↑: SRPC shifts UP — same unemployment, higher inflation.

Friedman-Phelps: No long-run trade-off. Attempts to keep u below u* accelerate inflation.

Stagflation (1970s): Supply shocks moved curve outward — both high u and high π simultaneously.

Solow Growth Model PhD

Long-run growth driven by capital accumulation, labour growth, and technology. The model predicts convergence to a steady-state capital stock where investment = depreciation.

Solow Steady State

Capital per Worker · Steady State · Golden Rule · Convergence
f(k) output per worker
s·f(k) investment
(δ+n)k break-even
Steady state k*

⚙ Variables

Savings rate (s) 0.30
Depreciation (δ) 0.08
Pop. growth (n) 0.02
Steady State k*
—
Δk = s·f(k) − (δ+n)·k
Steady state:
s·f(k*) = (δ+n)·k*
s↑: Investment curve shifts up → higher k* and y*. But no long-run growth rate change.

Golden Rule: s that maximises consumption per worker c* = f(k*)−(δ+n)k*.

Long-run growth requires technology (TFP) growth — the "residual" in growth accounting.

Laffer Curve M.Com

Tax revenue first rises then falls as the tax rate increases. Beyond the revenue-maximising rate, higher taxes reduce incentives and shrink the tax base.

Tax Revenue vs Tax Rate

Revenue Maximisation · Supply-side · Deadweight Loss · Tax Base
Tax Revenue
Current Rate
Max Revenue Point

⚙ Variables

Tax Rate (t%) 40
Revenue-max rate (t*%) 50
Revenue & Position
—
At t=0%: revenue = 0. At t=100%: revenue = 0 (no one works).
Peak at t* (revenue-maximising rate).

Left of t*: Supply-siders argue: cut taxes → revenue rises (incentive effect dominates).
Right of t*: Higher rate still raises revenue but approaches Laffer peak.

Empirical t* debated: often estimated 60-70% for top income taxes. Used to justify Reagan-era cuts.

Mundell-Fleming Model PhD

IS-LM extended to open economy. BP curve represents Balance of Payments equilibrium. Exchange rate regime changes policy effectiveness dramatically.

IS-LM-BP (Open Economy)

IS · LM · BP Curve · Capital Mobility · Exchange Rate Policy

BP

⚙ Variables

Fiscal Policy (IS shift) 0
Monetary Policy (LM shift) 0
Capital Mobility Medium
Policy Effectiveness
—
Fixed ER + High capital mobility: Fiscal policy effective; Monetary policy ineffective.
Floating ER + High capital mobility: Monetary policy effective; Fiscal policy ineffective (exchange rate offsets).

BP steep (low mobility): Capital doesn't flow freely; trade balance dominates.
BP flat (high mobility): r fixed by world rate; any deviation triggers capital flows.

Trilemma: Can't simultaneously have fixed ER + free capital + independent monetary policy.
